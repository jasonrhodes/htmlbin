Hypothesis

Bigger PRs get less review per line

Absolute comment count still rises with size — but comments per 100 lines added collapses. Reviewers don’t scale linearly with diff size. That’s the engagement-density problem.

Spearman rank correlation, comments/100add vs additions: ρ ≈ −0.76 (strong negative).

Time to merge

Size raises TTM — not linearly

Additions ↔ ready→merge: ρ ≈ 0.35 (moderate). Median TTM crawls from ~2h (tiny) to ~23h (500–999), then steps up past 1,000 lines (54h → 78h). Means are much higher than medians — a few stuck PRs dominate.

What actually predicts merge time

Discussion beats diff size

Comments ↔ ready→merge: ρ ≈ 0.75 — much stronger than size alone. Big quiet PRs can still merge relatively fast; heavily discussed PRs (of any size) sit longer. Size matters; review friction matters more.

Quiet giants

14% of ≥1k-add PRs have ≤2 comments

Among 355 PRs with ≥1,000 additions: median 23 comments, but 21% have zero review comments and 14% have ≤2 comments total. Those are the “large and quiet” merges — highest risk that size outran scrutiny.

Takeaways

  • Always show mean and median. Median says “typical”; mean says “burden of the tail.”
  • Exclude bots before judging team habits — they crush the all-repo median.
  • alerting_v2 is bigger than baseline (~3.5× median adds) but not absurd at the median; watch the ≥1k share (21%).
  • Review density falls as PRs grow — the real “out of control” signal.
  • TTM tracks discussion more than lines — split PRs help size; process helps friction.
